# Inclusion/Exclusion Criteria Framework

## Purpose

A reference guide for defining rigorous, pre-specified inclusion and exclusion criteria before executing a systematic literature search. Criteria must be defined before searching — post-hoc criteria introduce bias.

---

## The PICO(S) Framework

Use PICO(S) to structure criteria for empirical research:

| Element | Definition | Example |
|---------|-----------|---------|
| **P** — Population | Who is studied? | Adults with Type 2 diabetes |
| **I** — Intervention | What is done? | Mindfulness-based stress reduction |
| **C** — Comparison | Compared to what? | Standard care / no intervention |
| **O** — Outcome | What is measured? | HbA1c levels, quality of life |
| **S** — Study design | What methods? | RCTs, controlled trials |

For non-intervention research, adapt:
- **P** — Phenomenon of interest
- **I** — Issue/concept being examined
- **C** — Context or setting
- **O** — Outcome or measure of interest

---

## Standard Criteria Categories

### 1. Relevance

**Include**:
- Directly addresses the research question or one of its sub-questions
- Covers the population/phenomenon of interest
- Measures outcomes relevant to the RQ

**Exclude**:
- Tangential mention only (topic appears but is not the focus)
- Different population, setting, or phenomenon
- No relevant outcome data

### 2. Publication Type

**Options** (choose based on RQ):
- Include: Peer-reviewed journal articles only
- Include: Peer-reviewed + grey literature (government reports, WHO publications, etc.)
- Include: Conference papers (specify: peer-reviewed only vs. all)
- Exclude: Editorials, letters, commentaries (unless explicitly collecting expert opinion)
- Exclude: Book chapters (or include — specify)
- Include/Exclude: Preprints — must be explicitly decided and documented

### 3. Study Design

**Specify the minimum acceptable study design**:

| If RQ asks about... | Minimum design |
|---------------------|---------------|
| Intervention effectiveness | RCT or quasi-experimental |
| Prevalence/incidence | Cross-sectional or cohort |
| Risk factors | Cohort or case-control |
| Patient/user experience | Qualitative study |
| Concept/theory | Theoretical/conceptual paper |
| Diagnostic accuracy | Diagnostic study |
| Policy effectiveness | Evaluative study or systematic review |

### 4. Date Range

Common choices (document rationale for each):

| Range | Use when |
|-------|----------|
| Last 5 years | Rapidly evolving field (AI, social media, emerging diseases) |
| Last 10 years | Moderately evolving field; good balance of currency and coverage |
| Last 20 years | Established field; want to capture evolving practice |
| No date limit | Historical question; seminal papers may be old |
| Custom range | Specific policy period; era-defined research question |

**Always include**: Seminal papers regardless of date (cite reason for exception)

### 5. Language

**Include**:
- English (default)
- [Add languages if researcher has reading competency]

**Exclude**:
- Languages researcher cannot read (without translation access)

**Note**: Language restriction can introduce bias toward English-speaking countries. Document as a limitation.

### 6. Geographic Scope

**Options**:
- No restriction (global)
- Specific countries or regions (justify)
- Developed/developing country distinction (justify)
- Jurisdiction-specific (for legal or policy research)

### 7. Publication Status

**Decide explicitly**:
- Published only (avoids publication bias, but may miss recent work)
- Published + preprints (more current, but less reliable)
- Published + grey literature (reduces publication bias, adds policy relevance)
- Unpublished data (for meta-analyses to reduce publication bias — complex, requires protocols)

## Documenting Criteria

Before searching, write out your criteria in this format:

```
INCLUSION CRITERIA (all must be met):
IC1: [criterion]
IC2: [criterion]
IC3: [criterion]

EXCLUSION CRITERIA (any one triggers exclusion):
EC1: [criterion]
EC2: [criterion]
EC3: [criterion]
```

**Good criteria are**:
- Specific and measurable (not vague like "relevant")
- Binary (yes/no decision possible from abstract)
- Defined before seeing results
- Consistently applicable by different reviewers

**Bad criteria examples**:
- "High-quality studies" (not measurable without definition)
- "Recent literature" (not specific)
- "Studies that support our hypothesis" (selection bias)

## Example Criteria Sets

### Example 1: Clinical Intervention Study

**Topic**: Effectiveness of telehealth for managing hypertension in older adults

```
INCLUSION CRITERIA:
IC1: Population — adults aged 60+ with diagnosed hypertension (ICD-10: I10)
IC2: Intervention — telehealth delivery (video, phone, app-based monitoring)
IC3: Outcome — at least one blood pressure measure (systolic, diastolic, or both)
IC4: Study design — RCT, quasi-experimental, or cohort study
IC5: Publication year — 2015 to present (telehealth adoption before 2015 was minimal)
IC6: Language — English, Spanish (team competency)

EXCLUSION CRITERIA:
EC1: Pediatric population (< 18 years)
EC2: Study conducted in ICU or acute care setting
EC3: No control group or comparator
EC4: Conference abstract only (no full paper available)
EC5: Duplicate publication of same trial (retain primary report)
```

### Example 2: Education Research

**Topic**: Impact of gamification on student motivation in secondary education

```
INCLUSION CRITERIA:
IC1: Population — secondary school students (grades 6–12, ages 11–18)
IC2: Intervention — gamification elements (points, badges, leaderboards, levels)
IC3: Outcome — motivation measure (validated scale or behavioral indicator)
IC4: Setting — formal school environment (classroom or school-based)
IC5: Study design — any empirical design (quantitative, qualitative, mixed methods)
IC6: Publication year — 2010 to present
IC7: Language — English

EXCLUSION CRITERIA:
EC1: Higher education or adult learners
EC2: Informal/extracurricular settings only
EC3: Full gamification (complete game) rather than gamification elements
EC4: No motivation outcome measured
EC5: Opinion pieces, editorials, or non-empirical commentary
```

### Example 3: Social Sciences / Policy

**Topic**: Social determinants of vaccine hesitancy in high-income countries

```
INCLUSION CRITERIA:
IC1: Addresses vaccine hesitancy, refusal, or delayed uptake
IC2: Examines at least one social determinant (socioeconomic, cultural, political, information environment)
IC3: Setting — high-income country (World Bank classification)
IC4: Publication year — 2019 to present (COVID-19 era central to question)
IC5: Study types — surveys, qualitative studies, mixed methods, systematic reviews
IC6: Language — English, French, German (team competency)

EXCLUSION CRITERIA:
EC1: Vaccine hesitancy about specific vaccines studied only for clinical (not social) reasons
EC2: Low- or middle-income country settings
EC3: Clinical/immunological studies without social determinant component
EC4: Opinion/editorial pieces
```

## Handling Borderline Cases

When a source is borderline on any criterion:
1. Do not make an ad-hoc decision
2. Check whether the criterion definition covers this case
3. If criterion is ambiguous, **include** at Pass 1 (err toward inclusion)
4. Resolve at Pass 2 with full text
5. If still uncertain, note as "borderline — retained" or "borderline — excluded (reason)"

**Track borderline decisions**: They reveal criteria that need tightening for future searches.

## Common Biases in Inclusion/Exclusion

| Bias | Description | Prevention |
|------|-------------|-----------|
| Confirmation bias | Excluding studies that contradict expected findings | Define criteria before searching; blind screening if possible |
| Language bias | Restricting to English over-represents Western countries | Note as limitation; consider translation for high-impact non-English sources |
| Publication bias | Only including published studies misses null results | Actively search grey literature and preprints |
| Citation bias | Only including highly-cited sources | Do not use citation count as a criterion |
| Recency bias | Excluding older seminal works | Allow exceptions for seminal papers; justify |
